% ABSTRACT
% ============================================================================
\begin{abstract}
Predicting short-term mid-price movements in Limit Order Books (LOBs) is
a fundamentally difficult financial engineering problem characterised by
extreme non-stationarity, microstructure noise, and severe class imbalance.
This work introduces a complete Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) pipeline on the
FI-2010 benchmark dataset, combining three structurally diverse classifiers
managed via a learned Gating Network, a cost-aware execution state machine,
and an online Backtracking module. 

Our findings demonstrate that while the MoE does not significantly improve
raw mean return over its best single expert (XGBoost), it provides superior
risk-adjusted performance. Across 9 chronological folds, the Gated MoE
halves maximum drawdown (11.7\% vs 22.7\%), improves median session return
2.4$\times$ (+7.8\% vs +3.2\%), and achieves a higher Sortino ratio
(0.123 vs 0.097). Gating diagnostics reveal the network independently
discovered a routing logic assigning $\sim$78\% weight to XGBoost for
execution conviction, while using the Temporal MLP (macro-F1 0.562) as a
22\% regime filter to dampen over-trading. Furthermore, we report a
significant negative finding: the adaptive Backtracking module, while
increasing mean returns, systematically degrades win rates and decision
accuracy by loosening execution thresholds, indicating that online
adaptation is destabilising on short horizons. This work validates the
MoE architecture as a tool for risk-aware capital deployment rather than
raw return maximisation.
\end{abstract}

% 1. INTRODUCTION
% ============================================================================
\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Problem Context}
Modern financial markets are dominated by algorithmic trading protocols
operating at millisecond latencies. The fundamental data structure governing
price discovery is the Limit Order Book (LOB) --- a real-time sequential
ledger matching standing buy (Bid) and sell (Ask) orders at discrete price
levels. Predicting the short-term directional movement of the mid-price
(the average of the best bid and best ask) is the canonical problem in
market microstructure research.

The core challenge is extreme noise. Unlike macroscopic asset allocation,
high-frequency signals decay in seconds, and mid-price trajectories often
revert due to phantom liquidity, transient order cancellations, or
adversarial spoofing. Single-model approaches are notoriously susceptible
to overfitting against this localised variance.

\subsection{The Classification-Profitability Paradox}
To manage the difficulty of predicting exact scalar returns $R_t$,
the literature adopts a categorical formulation: mapping the LOB state to
three discrete classes --- \textit{Up}, \textit{Stationary}, \textit{Down}.
However, this introduces a fundamental paradox. A model trained via
standard cross-entropy loss structurally gravitates toward the majority
class (Stationary), which comprises $\sim$40\% of FI-2010 labels. While
predicting Stationary aggressively yields high classification accuracy,
it paralyses the trading agent: the model rarely builds enough
probabilistic conviction to breach the execution threshold
$\tau_{entry}=0.60$ required for capital deployment. A model achieving
90\% accuracy may extract 0\% financial return.

\subsection{Our Approach}
We solve this paradox by decoupling the classification mechanism from
the financial execution mechanism through a five-stage pipeline:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Data Loading \& Feature Engineering:} Raw DecPre LOB extraction with engineered microstructure indicators.
    \item \textbf{Expert Training:} Independent training of three diverse classifiers with hyperparameter tuning.
    \item \textbf{Gating Network:} Neural routing that learns contextual soft-weighting between experts.
    \item \textbf{Signal Strategy Layer:} A cost-aware state machine that converts probabilities into executable trades.
    \item \textbf{Online Backtracking:} Dynamic threshold adjustment designed for online parameter search.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Key Findings and Scientific Contributions}
Contrary to naive expectations, our results reveal that the Mixture-of-Experts
architecture's primary value lies in \textit{risk-adjusted stability}
rather than raw alpha generation. By dampening the aggressive conviction of
XGBoost with the conservative bias of a Temporal MLP, the MoE architecture
halves maximum drawdown and triples the median return over typical market
sessions. Furthermore, we report a critical negative finding regarding our
Backtracking module: online threshold adaptation increases trade frequency
but degrades precision, suggesting that the non-stationarity of LOB
microstructure makes rapid threshold search destabilising. This honest
accounting of both ensembling gains and adaptation failures provides a
rigorous template for future LOB trading research.

\section{Dataset Configuration and Preprocessing}

\subsection{The FI-2010 Benchmark}
We utilise the FI-2010 benchmark LOB dataset \cite{b1}, the academic
standard for microstructure forecasting. The corpus encapsulates 10
consecutive trading days of Finnish stock market data, comprising
approximately 400,000 tick events with full visibility across the outer
10 price levels of the order book.

Each raw data file has shape $(149, N_{samples})$ --- transposed relative
to the usual convention. Rows 0--143 contain 144 features; rows 144--148
contain labels for prediction horizons $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 5, 10\}$. Our
data loader transposes to $(N_{samples}, 149)$ and extracts the $k=10$
horizon label (row index 148) as the primary target.

\subsection{Raw Decimal Precision Extraction}
The FI-2010 benchmark provides data normalised via Z-Score, MinMax, and
Decimal Precision (DecPre) variants. We force extraction of the raw
\texttt{NoAuction\_DecPre} matrices because Z-Score normalisation destroys
the strict positivity of volume integers, mathematically corrupting
non-linear ratio engineering (e.g., relative volume imbalances require
physically positive denominators). By evaluating absolute quantities, we
guarantee mathematically valid fractional inputs.

\subsection{Feature Engineering (144 $\rightarrow$ 147 Dimensions)}
Three high-conviction microstructure features are computed from the raw
DecPre data and appended to the 144 baseline features:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Level-1 Spread} ($S_t$): The instantaneous cost of crossing liquidity.
    \begin{equation}
        S_t = \text{AskPrice}_1 - \text{BidPrice}_1
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Level-1 Order Imbalance} ($I_t^{(1)}$): Normalised immediate buying vs.\ selling pressure, bounded in $[-1, 1]$.
    \begin{equation}
        I_t^{(1)} = \frac{\text{BidVol}_1 - \text{AskVol}_1}{\text{BidVol}_1 + \text{AskVol}_1 + \epsilon}
    \end{equation}

    \item \textbf{Level-5 Deep Imbalance} ($I_t^{(5)}$): Cumulative imbalance across the outer 5 price tiers, designed to nullify Level-1 spoofing noise.
    \begin{equation}
        I_t^{(5)} = \frac{\sum_{l=1}^{5}\text{BidVol}_l - \sum_{l=1}^{5}\text{AskVol}_l}{\sum_{l=1}^{5}\text{BidVol}_l + \sum_{l=1}^{5}\text{AskVol}_l + \epsilon}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

After feature engineering, the complete 147-dimensional matrix is
Z-score normalised: \texttt{StandardScaler} is fit on training data
and applied to both train and test sets to prevent information leakage.

\subsection{Label Encoding and Class Weights}
Original FI-2010 labels are in $\{1, 2, 3\}$; we convert to
$\{0, 1, 2\}$ representing Up, Stationary, Down respectively.
Balanced class weights are computed via \texttt{sklearn.compute\_class\_weight}
using the ``balanced'' strategy, which assigns higher weight to
underrepresented classes. These weights are passed to all expert
loss functions to counteract class imbalance.

\subsection{Cross-Validation Protocol}
We use \textbf{Anchored Forward Time-Series Cross-Validation} with 9
chronologically sequential folds. Fold $t$ trains on all data up to
day $t$ and tests on the data from day $t+1$. This strictly prevents
temporal data leakage --- no future information is ever used during
training. Training set sizes range from 39,512 (Fold 1) to 288,504
(Fold 9) samples, while test sets range from 20,000 to 38,000 samples.

% ============================================================================
% 3. EXPERT ARCHITECTURES
% ============================================================================
\section{Expert Architectures}

Our MoE framework combines three structurally diverse classifiers. The
key design principle is \textit{model diversity}: each expert exploits
fundamentally different inductive biases to ensure uncorrelated prediction
errors. All experts output calibrated 3-class probability vectors
$P_i(X_t) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ summing to 1.

\subsection{Expert 1: L2-Regularised Logistic Regression}
A multinomial logistic regression model with L2 penalty serves as the
linear baseline. It possesses zero capacity for feature interaction
discovery, forcing its weights to exploit the engineered microstructure
features ($I_t^{(1)}$, $I_t^{(5)}$, $S_t$) directly.

\textbf{Hyperparameter tuning:} The regularisation strength $C$ is
selected from $\{0.01, 0.1, 1.0, 10.0\}$ via 3-fold stratified
cross-validation maximising macro-F1 on training data. The model is
retrained on the full training set with the best $C$.

\textbf{Implementation:} \texttt{sklearn.LogisticRegression} with LBFGS
solver, max\_iter=1000, balanced class weights.

\textbf{Role in MoE:} Provides a stable, low-variance probability
baseline. The gating network learns to assign $<0.1\%$ weight to LR,
effectively zeroing it out --- confirming that linear separability is
insufficient for LOB prediction.

\subsection{Expert 2: XGBoost Gradient-Boosted Decision Trees}
XGBoost exploits the tabular structure of LOB data through gradient-boosted
boolean splits. Decision trees inherently outperform sequential deep
learning on tabular data by mapping conditional feature interactions
without normalisation requirements.

\textbf{Architecture:} \texttt{XGBClassifier} with \texttt{multi:softprob}
objective, 500 estimators, stochastic subsampling (80\% of rows, 80\%
of columns per tree), and histogram-based tree construction.

\textbf{Hyperparameter tuning:} Grid search over max\_depth $\in \{3, 4, 5\}$
and learning\_rate $\in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.1\}$ via 3-fold stratified CV on
macro-F1. Sample-level weights are computed from balanced class weights.

\textbf{Key property:} XGBoost produces \textit{high-conviction}
probability outputs. Its split-based architecture naturally produces
extreme probability vectors (e.g., $[0.85, 0.10, 0.05]$) that consistently
exceed the $\tau_{entry}=0.60$ execution threshold, enabling 280+ trades
per fold. This is the critical mechanism behind its financial profitability.

\subsection{Expert 3: Temporal Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP)}

\subsubsection{Temporal Windowing}
Unlike decision trees, neural networks benefit from sequential context.
We implement a temporal lookback mechanism with $k_{lookback}=5$: the
current timestep's 147 features are horizontally concatenated with the
preceding 4 timesteps, producing an input dimension of:
\begin{equation}
    D_{MLP} = 147 \times 5 = 735
\end{equation}
The first 4 timesteps are padded by replicating the initial observation.
This gives the MLP access to short-term momentum and mean-reversion
patterns invisible to the single-timestep experts.

\subsubsection{Network Architecture}
\begin{equation*}
\text{Input}(735) \rightarrow \text{Linear}(256) \rightarrow \text{BN} \rightarrow \text{ReLU} \rightarrow \text{Dropout}(0.3)
\end{equation*}
\begin{equation*}
\rightarrow \text{Linear}(128) \rightarrow \text{BN} \rightarrow \text{ReLU} \rightarrow \text{Dropout}(0.3) \rightarrow \text{Linear}(3)
\end{equation*}

Batch Normalisation stabilises gradient flow across the wide input;
Dropout (30\%) prevents co-adaptation. The network outputs raw logits;
softmax is applied externally during inference.

\subsubsection{Focal Loss ($\gamma=3.0$)}
To counteract the profound class imbalance toward Stationary targets,
the MLP is trained with Focal Loss \cite{b2}:
\begin{equation}
    FL(p_t) = -\alpha_t (1 - p_t)^\gamma \log(p_t)
\end{equation}
where $\gamma=3.0$ aggressively down-weights well-classified (easy)
examples and magnifies error penalties on hard, minority directional
samples. Class weights $\alpha_t$ are the balanced weights computed from
training label frequencies.

\subsubsection{Training Protocol}
Adam optimiser with $lr=10^{-3}$, batch size 256, up to 50 epochs with
early stopping (patience=5 epochs) on validation macro-F1. A 10\%
stratified split is held out from training data for validation. The best
model state (by validation F1) is restored after training.

\subsubsection{The MLP Paradox}
Despite achieving the highest classification accuracy (57.2\% $\pm$ 3.6\%)
and Macro-F1 (56.2\% $\pm$ 3.3\%), the MLP generates only $+0.2\%$ mean
return. \textit{Why?} Focal Loss with high $\gamma$ trains the network
to be cautious --- its probability outputs are ``soft'' and rarely exceed
the $\tau_{entry}=0.60$ execution threshold. It averages only 9.6 trades
per fold across $\sim$38,000 test samples. The few trades it does attempt
are caught in microstructure latency traps, bleeding to transaction costs.
This is the core of the Classification-Profitability Paradox.

% 4. MIXTURE-OF-EXPERTS ARCHITECTURE
% ============================================================================
\section{Mixture-of-Experts Architecture}

\subsection{The MoE Equation}
The MoE framework independently evaluates all three experts on the input
state $X_t$, then combines their outputs via learned gating weights. The
final probability vector is:
\begin{equation}
    P_{final}(X_t) = \sum_{i=1}^{3} G_i(X_t) \cdot E_i(X_t)
\end{equation}
where $E_i(X_t) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the $i$-th expert's probability
output and $G_i(X_t) \in [0,1]$ is the gating weight satisfying
$\sum_i G_i(X_t) = 1$.

\subsection{Stacked Probability Generation (Stage 2)}
To prevent data leakage when training the gating network, we generate
\textit{cross-validated stacked probabilities} on the training set using
3-fold inner stratified cross-validation. For each inner fold:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Train fresh copies of all 3 experts on the inner training split.
    \item Generate probability predictions on the inner validation split.
    \item Store these out-of-fold predictions.
\end{enumerate}
This produces unbiased probability estimates for every training sample,
which are then used to train the gating network. Test-set probabilities
are generated from the full-data experts trained in Stage 1.

\subsection{Gating Network Architecture (Stage 3)}
The gating network is a lightweight neural network that takes the
concatenated feature vector and expert probabilities as input:
\begin{equation*}
    \text{Input} = [X_t \in \mathbb{R}^{147}, \; P_{LR} \in \mathbb{R}^3, \; P_{XGB} \in \mathbb{R}^3, \; P_{MLP} \in \mathbb{R}^3]
\end{equation*}
This 156-dimensional input is StandardScaler-normalised and fed through:
\begin{equation*}
    \text{Linear}(156 \rightarrow 64) \rightarrow \text{BN} \rightarrow \text{ReLU} \rightarrow \text{Dropout}(0.2) \rightarrow \text{Linear}(64 \rightarrow 3) \rightarrow \text{Softmax}
\end{equation*}

\textbf{Training objective:} The gating network minimises the negative
log-likelihood of the gating-weighted expert combination:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L} = -\sum_{t} \log\left(\sum_{i=1}^{3} G_i(X_t) \cdot E_i(X_t)\right)[y_t]
\end{equation}
Trained with Adam ($lr=10^{-3}$, weight\_decay=$10^{-4}$), batch size 256,
for 30 epochs.

\subsection{Gating Network Diagnostics}
Post-training analysis reveals the gating network's learned routing
behaviour. Across 9 folds:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mean soft weights:} LR: 0.09\% $\pm$ 0.04\%, XGB: 78.0\% $\pm$ 13.8\%, MLP: 21.9\% $\pm$ 13.8\%
    \item \textbf{Argmax routing:} LR: 0.0\%, XGB: 77.7\%, MLP: 22.3\%
    \item \textbf{Dominant expert:} XGBoost
\end{itemize}

The gating network empirically learns to route $\sim$78\% of predictions
through XGBoost while using MLP 22\% of the time as a regime filter.
LR is effectively zeroed out. This is a genuine finding: the network
independently discovers that XGBoost's conviction structure is essential
for financial execution, while MLP's conservative bias provides value as
a dampening signal in uncertain microstructure regimes.

% 5. SIGNAL STRATEGY LAYER
% ============================================================================
\section{Algorithmic Trading State Machine}

Raw probability vectors $P_{final}(X_t)$ are inherently meaningless without
execution constraints. Trading entails transaction costs, position holding
durations, and regime-dependent execution quality. We construct a
deterministic Signal Strategy Layer with localised hysteresis.

\subsection{Conviction Hysteresis}
The directional signal is defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \text{signal}_t = P_{up}(X_t) - P_{down}(X_t)
\end{equation}

Rather than executing on $\arg\max$, trading requires the signal to eclipse
the stationary null hypothesis by a rigid magnitude offset. The state
machine operates as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item If $|\text{signal}_t| > \tau_{entry}$ AND $P_{up} > \theta_{buy}$ $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Buy}
    \item If $|\text{signal}_t| > \tau_{entry}$ AND $P_{down} > \theta_{sell}$ $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Sell}
    \item If $|\text{signal}_t| < \tau_{exit}$ AND in position $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Flat} (exit)
    \item Otherwise $\rightarrow$ \textbf{Hold} (maintain current state)
\end{enumerate}

Default thresholds: $\tau_{entry}=0.60$, $\tau_{exit}=0.15$,
$\theta_{buy}=\theta_{sell}=0.75$.

\subsection{Flip Penalty}
Position reversals (Long $\rightarrow$ Short or vice versa) require
stronger conviction than fresh entries:
\begin{equation}
    |\text{signal}_t| > \tau_{entry} + \Delta_{flip} \quad (\Delta_{flip}=0.10)
\end{equation}
This prevents churning in oscillating regimes where the signal alternates
around the entry threshold.

\subsection{20-Step Regime Filter}
The most financially corrosive market structure is the ``sideways chop'' ---
stochastic flickering that randomly triggers entry thresholds but fails
to sustain momentum. We implement a 20-step sliding-window regime filter:
\begin{equation}
    s_t = \max(P_{up}(X_t), P_{down}(X_t))
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    \text{Regime valid iff} \quad \frac{1}{20}\sum_{i=0}^{19} s_{t-i} > 0.55
\end{equation}
All directional execution is physically halted unless the sequence
demonstrates sustained directional pressure. The first 20 timesteps
of each fold are a warm-up period where no trades are executed.

\subsection{Minimum Holding Period}
Once a position is opened, a $k_{hold}=25$ tick lockout prevents
immediate exit. This ensures each trade has sufficient time to capture
directional momentum and prevents transaction-cost-dominated round trips.

\section{Backtesting Engine}

\subsection{Portfolio Simulation}
The backtesting engine simulates a hypothetical trading account starting
with \$10,000 initial capital. It processes the action sequence from the
strategy layer and applies realised mid-price returns:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Buy:} Opens a Long position. Portfolio earns $r_t$ each tick.
    \item \textbf{Sell:} Opens a Short position. Portfolio earns $-r_t$ each tick.
    \item \textbf{Flat:} Closes any open position.
    \item \textbf{Hold:} Maintains the current position (or stays flat).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Transaction Cost Model}
Each position entry and exit incurs a proportional cost of $c$ applied
multiplicatively: $V \leftarrow V \times (1 - c)$. The default cost is
$c = 1$ basis point ($10^{-4}$), consistent with institutional market
access fees. Position transitions (e.g., Long $\rightarrow$ Short) incur
double cost (close + open).

\subsection{Mid-Price Return Computation}
Real mid-prices are loaded from the raw DecPre files:
\begin{equation}
    m_t = \frac{\text{AskPrice}_{1,t} + \text{BidPrice}_{1,t}}{2}
\end{equation}
One-step returns are computed as $r_t = (m_{t+1} - m_t) / m_t$.

\subsection{Performance Metrics}
We compute the following metrics on each fold:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Cumulative Return:} $(V_T - V_0) / V_0$
    \item \textbf{Sortino Ratio:} $\bar{r} / \sigma_{down}$, measuring return per unit of downside risk only. Returns 0 if no downside volatility exists.
    \item \textbf{Maximum Drawdown (MaxDD):} Largest peak-to-trough decline as a fraction of peak value.
    \item \textbf{Win Rate:} Fraction of completed trades with positive return.
    \item \textbf{Decision Accuracy:} Fraction of Buy/Sell actions where the true direction matched (Buy$\rightarrow$Up, Sell$\rightarrow$Down).
    \item \textbf{Profit Factor:} Gross profit / gross loss across all trades.
    \item \textbf{Turnover:} Fraction of timesteps with Buy or Sell actions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Benchmark Strategies}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Buy \& Hold:} $\prod(1 + r_t) - 1$. Captures entire market trend with zero transaction costs.
    \item \textbf{Passive Signal (Oracle):} Perfect directional knowledge --- Long when true label is Up, Short when Down, flat when Stationary. This is a theoretical upper bound, not a realistic benchmark.
\end{itemize}

% 7. ADAPTIVE BACKTRACKING MODULE
% ============================================================================
\section{Adaptive Backtracking Module}

\subsection{Motivation}
Static execution thresholds ($\tau_{entry}$, $\theta_{buy}$, $\theta_{sell}$)
are suboptimal because market microstructure conditions change within each
test fold. A threshold that works well in trending regimes may cause
excessive false positives in mean-reverting regimes.

\subsection{Architecture}
The Backtracking (BT) module operates \textit{online during inference}.
It maintains a rolling memory buffer of the last $N_{buf}=100$ observations,
storing $(y_{pred}, P_{final}, \text{action}, y_{true})$ tuples. Every
$N_{upd}=50$ steps, it computes error rates and adjusts thresholds.

\subsection{Threshold Adaptation Logic}
For each update window, we compute:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Buy False Positive Rate:} Action was Buy but true was not Up.
    \item \textbf{Buy False Negative Rate:} True was Up but action was not Buy.
    \item (Symmetric computation for Sell/Down.)
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Conviction thresholds ($\theta_{buy}$, $\theta_{sell}$):}
\begin{equation}
    \theta \leftarrow \begin{cases}
        \min(\theta + \epsilon, 0.85) & \text{if FP rate} > \tau_{FP} \\
        \max(\theta - \epsilon, 0.45) & \text{if FN rate} > \tau_{FN}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Primary execution gate ($\tau_{entry}$, $\tau_{exit}$):}
\begin{equation}
    \tau_{entry} \leftarrow \begin{cases}
        \min(\tau_{entry} + \epsilon, 0.80) & \text{if combined FP} > 2\tau_{FP} \\
        \max(\tau_{entry} - \epsilon, 0.40) & \text{if combined FN} > 2\tau_{FN}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

Default hyperparameters: $\epsilon=0.02$, $\tau_{FP}=0.15$, $\tau_{FN}=0.10$.
Thresholds are clamped to prevent degenerate values.

\subsection{Reversal Override}
If the previous action was Buy and $P_{down} > \theta_{reverse}=0.70$,
the current action is overridden to Hold. This prevents executing into
a strong reversal signal.

\subsection{Wiring}
At each timestep, the BT module's adjusted thresholds are written back
to the StrategyLayer \textit{before} the decision is made:
$\tau_{entry}$, $\tau_{exit}$, $\theta_{buy}$, $\theta_{sell}$ are all
propagated. This ensures the BT module genuinely affects execution.

% 8. CONFIDENCE-BASED ROUTING (ABLATION)
% ============================================================================
\section{Confidence-Based Routing (CR) Ablation}

To validate that the learned gating network provides genuine value beyond
simple heuristics, we implement a confidence-based routing baseline:

\begin{equation}
    P_{final}(X_t) = \begin{cases}
        P_{XGB}(X_t) & \text{if } \max(P_{XGB}) > 0.60 \\
        P_{MLP}(X_t) & \text{elif } \max(P_{MLP}) > 0.60 \\
        P_{XGB}(X_t) & \text{otherwise (fallback)}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}

This produces a ``MoE+CR'' variant that bypasses the gating network
entirely when an expert is highly confident. If confidence routing
matches or exceeds the learned gating, the gating network adds no value.
If it underperforms, the learned gating captures inter-expert dynamics
that naive heuristics cannot.

The MLP's conservative probability outputs rarely breach

This is the core empirical evidence for the paradox: \textit{classification
accuracy and financial profitability are decoupled in LOB trading}.

\subsection{Gating Network Diagnostics}
The learned gating network assigns:
\begin{itemize}
    \item LR: 0.09\% $\pm$ 0.04\% (effectively zeroed)
    \item XGB: 78.0\% $\pm$ 13.8\% (dominant)
    \item MLP: 21.9\% $\pm$ 13.8\% (regime filter)
\end{itemize}
Under argmax routing, XGBoost is selected 77.7\% of the time. This
confirms the gating network independently discovers that XGBoost's
conviction is essential for execution. The MLP serves as a moderating
influence in uncertain regimes, preventing over-trading.

\subsection{Core Finding: MoE Risk-Adjusted Superiority}
The MoE does not improve raw return over XGB (+31.8\% vs +32.8\%,
not statistically significant). However, it provides clear
risk-adjusted improvement on every metric:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Maximum drawdown halved:} 11.7\% vs 22.7\% (-48\%).
    \item \textbf{Median return 2.4$\times$ higher:} +7.8\% vs +3.2\%.
    \item \textbf{Sortino ratio improved:} 0.123 vs 0.097.
    \item \textbf{Return volatility reduced:} $\pm$76.9\% vs $\pm$86.2\%.
\end{itemize}

The mechanism is transparent: the gating network routes 78\% of decisions
to XGBoost while using MLP's stationarity bias as a natural brake on
over-trading during weak regimes. The MLP component dampens aggressive
XGB entries in volatile microstructure conditions, preventing the
worst-case drawdowns.

\subsection{Honest Central Tendency: Median Return Analysis}
\textit{Median return, not mean, is the honest measure of typical
performance in high-variance strategies.} Standard deviations exceed
means for all active strategies (e.g., XGB: $+32.8\% \pm 86.2\%$),
meaning a small number of exceptional folds drive the positive mean.
The median reveals what happens in a \textit{typical} fold:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{XGB median:} +3.2\% --- barely break-even in most folds.
    \item \textbf{MoE median:} +7.8\% --- 2.4$\times$ higher than XGB.
    \item \textbf{Buy \& Hold median:} +31.1\% --- substantially above both active strategies.
\end{itemize}

The B\&H advantage requires an exposure caveat: Buy \& Hold is always
invested at zero transaction cost, capturing the full upward trend of
FI-2010 mid-prices. Active strategies are selectively invested with
per-trade costs, inherently disadvantaged in trending markets. The
relevant comparison is between active strategies, where MoE's 2.4$\times$
median advantage over XGB is the honest core result.

\subsection{Negative Result: Backtracking Degrades Execution Quality}
\label{sec_bt_negative}
The Backtracking (BT) module is reported as a \textbf{negative result}.
Although MoE+BT achieves the highest mean return (+60.7\%), this is
driven by increased variance ($\pm$120.5\%), \textit{not} by better
decisions. The evidence:

\begin{itemize}
    \item BT increases trade count by 41\% (397 vs 281).
    \item Win rate drops from 57.9\% to 55.2\%.
    \item Decision accuracy drops from 74.4\% to 67.1\%.
    \item Return std explodes from $\pm$76.9\% to $\pm$120.5\%.
\end{itemize}

The BT module's online $\tau_{entry}$ adaptation systematically loosens
the execution gate, generating more trades of lower quality. The higher
mean return is a statistical artefact of increased variance on a small
sample (9 folds), not evidence of genuinely superior execution. We
conclude that \textit{online threshold adaptation is destabilising on
this short horizon}, consistent with the known difficulty of online
learning in non-stationary financial environments.

\subsection{CR Collapse: Why Confidence Routing $\approx$ XGB}
MoE+CR achieves +32.4\% return with Sortino 0.096 --- statistically
indistinguishable from raw XGB (+32.8\%, Sortino 0.097). This is
\textit{expected}: since argmax routing already assigns 77.7\% of ticks
to XGB, and the CR heuristic routes to XGB whenever
$\max(P_{XGB}) > 0.60$ or as fallback, CR effectively collapses the
ensemble to raw XGB behaviour. The critical implication is that
\textit{naive confidence heuristics cannot replicate the risk reduction
achieved by the learned gating network} --- CR produces XGB-level
drawdown (22.8\%) while the Gated MoE achieves 11.7\%. The gating
network captures inter-expert dynamics (specifically, when to dampen
XGB with MLP) that threshold-based routing cannot.

\section{What This System Reveals About Reality}

\subsection{The Fundamental Tension}
Our system reveals a deep structural tension in algorithmic trading:
\textit{the model that best understands the market is not the model that
best trades it}. The MLP, trained with Focal Loss on temporal sequences,
genuinely learns more about market microstructure --- evidenced by its
superior F1. But its learned conservatism (predicting probabilities near
the decision boundary) makes it unsuitable for threshold-gated execution.

\subsection{Conviction Is the Currency of Execution}
XGBoost's financial success stems not from superior understanding but from
\textit{conviction structure}. Decision trees produce extreme probability
outputs by design --- each sample is routed through a sequence of boolean
splits that push probabilities toward 0 or 1. This architectural property,
which is a liability for calibrated uncertainty estimation, becomes an
asset for threshold-sensitive trading.

\subsection{Ensembling Can Destroy Alpha}
Naive ensemble averaging (equal-weight, F1-weighted) collapses returns to
near zero because averaging probability vectors dilutes directional
conviction below the execution threshold. The fundamental reason is mathematical:
if $P_{XGB} = [0.80, 0.10, 0.10]$ and $P_{MLP} = [0.40, 0.35, 0.25]$,
the average $[0.60, 0.225, 0.175]$ \textit{barely} crosses $\tau_{entry}$
and the signal $0.60 - 0.175 = 0.425$ falls below the required 0.60.
A learned gating network is architecturally necessary to avoid this collapse.

\subsection{Online Adaptation Is Destabilising}
The Backtracking module demonstrates that online threshold adaptation on
a 9-fold evaluation horizon is actively harmful. While BT achieves the
highest mean return (+60.7\%), it does so by trading 41\% more
frequently with 7.3 percentage points lower decision accuracy. The
higher return variance ($\pm$120.5\% vs $\pm$76.9\%) means the BT result
is less statistically reliable. This is a \textit{negative result worth
reporting}: it suggests that the $N_{buf}=100$, $N_{upd}=50$ update
schedule adapts too aggressively for the non-stationary microstructure,
systematically loosening $\tau_{entry}$ and generating low-quality
trades. More conservative adaptation (larger buffers, smaller $\epsilon$)
or regime-conditional gating may improve stability.

\section{Conclusion}

This work demonstrates that LOB mid-price prediction cannot be optimised
solely via classification accuracy. The core findings are:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Classification-Profitability Paradox is empirically
    supported:} The highest-F1 model (MLP, 56.2\%) achieves near-zero
    financial return while the lower-F1 XGBoost (+32.8\%) succeeds by
    maintaining execution conviction. Classification accuracy and trading
    profitability are decoupled in threshold-gated execution systems.

    \item \textbf{The Gated MoE provides genuine risk reduction:} It halves
    maximum drawdown (11.7\% vs 22.7\%), improves median return 2.4$\times$
    (+7.8\% vs +3.2\%), and achieves higher Sortino (0.123 vs 0.097).
    The gating network independently discovers that XGBoost (78\% weight)
    provides execution conviction while MLP (22\% weight) serves as a
    regime filter --- a non-trivial learned behaviour.

    \item \textbf{Backtracking is a negative result:} MoE+BT increases
    trade count by 41\% while degrading win rate and decision accuracy.
    The higher mean return (+60.7\%) is driven by increased variance,
    not better decisions. Online threshold adaptation is destabilising on
    this horizon.

    \item \textbf{Confidence routing collapses to XGB:} MoE+CR produces
    results statistically indistinguishable from raw XGB, confirming that
    naive heuristics cannot replicate the risk reduction achieved by
    learned gating.

    \item \textbf{Variance dominates signal:} All active strategies exhibit
    std $>$ mean. Median return is the honest central tendency. Buy \& Hold
    (median +31.1\%) outperforms both active strategies in a typical fold,
    though it carries constant market exposure at zero cost.
\end{enumerate}

The defensible narrative is: \textit{the MoE does not beat XGB on raw
return, but it provides risk-adjusted improvement that matters in
practice.} The gating network's learned routing is the strongest
contribution --- a non-trivial, data-driven discovery that validates
the MoE architecture over fixed ensembles. These findings reinforce that
ensemble design for financial execution must account for
threshold-sensitive execution dynamics and conviction preservation,
not only classification objectives.
